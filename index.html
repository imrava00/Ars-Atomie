What is the chemistry olympiad?

The chemistry olympiad is a competitive exam that goes far beyond the school syllabus. Instead of recalling definitions, you apply chemical principles to unfamiliar problems, often under strict time pressure.

What makes it different

Questions are multi step and combine several ideas at once: a single problem might require balancing an equation, converting units, and reasoning about equilibrium in sequence. Depth matters more than coverage, and careful reading is rewarded as much as calculation.

What the exam looks like

Most rounds are multiple choice or short structured questions with a tight time limit. You will work with chemical formulas, equations, and data from experiments, which is exactly what the simulations here train: pacing, precision, and checking your work before submitting.

Core topics to master

The question bank on this site focuses on the areas most olympiad exams test first:

Stoichiometry

Mole calculations, limiting reagents, percent yield, and solution concentrations. The mechanical backbone of nearly every problem.

Atomic structure

Electron configuration, quantum numbers, and periodic trends in radius, ionization energy, and electronegativity.

Chemical bonding

Ionic and covalent bonding, Lewis structures, molecular geometry, and polarity.

Equilibrium and kinetics

Equilibrium constants, Le Chatelier's principle, reaction rates, and energy diagrams.

Acids and bases

pH calculations, buffers, titrations, and strength of acids and bases.

Redox and electrochemistry

Oxidation states, balancing redox reactions, and electrochemical cells.

How to prepare with this site

Three steps, in order. Repeat the cycle until your score stops improving; that is the sign you are ready for the next topic.

1
Study the material

Open the lecture notes and work through the sections your teacher published. Read them fully before attempting any simulation on that topic.

2
Practice with simulations

Open the simulations, pick one, enter your name, and answer. If there is a time limit, the clock runs exactly like the real contest, so practice your pacing, not just your knowledge.

3
Review every miss

After submitting, study the explanation for each wrong answer. Chemical formulas and equations are rendered properly in the review, so you can see exactly where your reasoning broke down. Then retake.

Frequently asked

Are the simulations graded?
Yes. Each question has a point value, and your total score is shown immediately after submitting, followed by a full review with the answer key.
Do I need an account?
No. Just enter your name before starting a simulation so your teacher can identify your result.
Where are my past results?
Results are saved in the browser you used, on that device. To keep a record, take a screenshot of your result page after submitting.
Can I retake a simulation?
Yes, as many times as you want. Retaking after reading the explanations is the most effective way to use this site.
Will I need to write chemical formulas during the simulation?
No. All questions are multiple choice. Formulas and equations appear as properly typeset chemistry, for example balanced reactions with subscripts and states, so you read them the way they would appear in a real paper.
The formulas look strange on my screen
The rendering library loads from the internet the first time a page opens. Reload the page once while online and formulas will display correctly.
